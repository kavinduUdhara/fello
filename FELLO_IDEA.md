# Fello — The Idea, In Plain English

## Where It Started

Kavindu was a member of an IEEE student branch. Every time they organized an event, the same exhausting cycle repeated itself. Documents would get made by the publicity team, sent to the design team, reviewed, sent back, changed, sent to the lead group, changed again. If one person was the design team lead, they were also in the publicity team, the lead team, and the common team with all members. The secretary was in all 8 WhatsApp groups for every single event.

And every event, someone had to sit down and manually create 8 WhatsApp groups, add the right people to each one, and then spend the next few weeks being the human router connecting all of them.

That was the starting point.

---

## The Core Insight

The organization already has a structure. Roles, teams, hierarchy, relationships. But that structure lives nowhere. It's not written down anywhere that a computer can understand. So every event, someone has to manually reconstruct it from scratch — who's in what team, who needs to see what, who approves what — all in WhatsApp groups and email chains.

If you encode that structure once, a lot of this collapses. The groups create themselves. The right people are already in the right places. The AI knows who owns what.

## The Research

Kavindu talked to six people from IEEE student branches and other university clubs. Here's what they said, in their own words:

**Shafkhan** said replying to messages takes most of his time. He has 5-6 teams and coordinates them by having each team report up to him through one group, while keeping separate direct lines open with each chairperson as a check. He switched to voice notes because typed messages need to be organized and voice notes don't.

**Sahanas** described finding speakers and sponsors for an event. She gets the percentage of delegates by specialization, searches LinkedIn for relevant people, checks their profiles one by one, and builds a list. Then she has a spreadsheet of 500+ companies. She calls each one, marks their reply, sends emails to those who ask for it, follows up, tracks everything. From 500+ companies they got 10. That's the process every time.

**Vishara** said finding sponsors is the hardest part. They make a spreadsheet, add companies, log the point of contact, note whether an email was sent, whether they heard back. For volunteers they make a post and "spam it on every WhatsApp group and call it a day hoping to god that people would be interested."

**Vihara** said constantly following up with people, waiting for responses, updating documents, and attending long meetings is exhausting, especially when simple communication could have solved the issue faster.

**Aneeshvara** said handling the delegation of tasks — assigning each individual task, giving it a timeline, checking the status — is difficult. He said if there was an AI where he could just speak to it and it automatically assigns tasks and makes deadlines, that would be great.

**Chathumina**, who oversees all IEEE activity at SLIIT, listed her biggest pain points as: accountability for completing work from subunit members, engaging inactive members, workload tracking — who is available and who has done what — and ensuring standard practices are followed by all committees.

She also said: "The only issue with that is sharing access to our emails and drives." That's the security concern that most AI tools can't answer. Fello can.

## The Problem, In Seven Lines

Manual follow-up is exhausting. Replying to messages takes most of the coordinator's time.

WhatsApp groups are rebuilt from scratch for every event. Eight groups, manually, every time.

Outreach is tracked in spreadsheets. One person called 500 companies and logged every response by hand.

Tasks get lost. Nobody knows who's doing what without asking.

Documents bounce between teams. Ask publicity, ask secretary, ask chair — deadline passes.

Files are scattered everywhere. Personal drives, WhatsApp, email attachments. Nobody knows what exists or where.

The same registration form gets rebuilt manually every event. Same fields. Every time.

---

## What Fello Does

Fello is not another project management tool. It's not asking people to change how they communicate. WhatsApp stays. The groups stay. The way people work stays.

What changes is that there's now an AI agent living inside those groups. It listens. It understands context. It handles the coordination work in the background so the humans can focus on the actual work.

The organization's structure — its teams, roles, hierarchy, relationships — gets encoded once. From that point on, the AI knows who everyone is and what they're responsible for.

When someone creates an event, the WhatsApp groups for that event create themselves. The right people are in the right groups automatically. Nobody has to think about it.

When someone uploads a file to a WhatsApp group, it gets saved to the right folder in Google Drive automatically. The AI confirms it's saved. Nobody has to chase it.

When someone asks "what's blocking this event" — the AI already knows. It's been in every group. It's seen every message. It gives a real answer in seconds.

When a task is done, someone just says "done" in the group. The AI marks it complete. The coordinator doesn't have to chase anyone.

When a follow-up needs to go out — to a sponsor, to a volunteer who hasn't joined the group yet, to a team member who missed a deadline — the AI handles it.

## The Story That Shows It Best

A vice chair drops a message in the IEEE IAS WhatsApp group: "guys we have a new event called PTI, need a logo."

Fello picks that up. Creates the PTI event. Asks: "Do you have a logo or shall we create one?"

Chair says: "let's create one."

Fello asks when they need it by. Chair says next Friday. Fello creates the task and sends a message to the IAS Design Team group: "Hey team, PTI needs a logo by next Friday. Anyone available?"

A designer says: "I can do it."

Fello asks if they can deliver by Friday. Designer says they need until Saturday. Fello negotiates — "The chair set Friday, can you do Friday evening?" Designer agrees. Fello assigns the task with that deadline and notifies the chair.

Later the designer says "logo is done" in the group. Fello marks the task complete and tells the chair.

Fello then asks: "Want me to create a volunteer registration form for PTI?" Chair says yes. Form is created automatically. Fello asks about interview slots. Calendar slots created. A volunteer interview WhatsApp group is created. Fello starts sending reminders to registered volunteers about their booked time slots.

The chair opens the Fello dashboard and types: "what's the status of PTI?" The AI responds with a full summary — tasks completed, what's in progress, what's overdue, who's responsible for what.

That whole story started with one WhatsApp message.

## The Org Structure

IEEE student branches have a specific hierarchy. There's the main branch with an executive committee — chair, vice chair, secretary, treasurer, webmaster, assistant secretary — and functional teams like editorial, public visibility, volunteer management, logistics, and finance.

Under the branch there are sub-branches — IAS, RAS, CS, PES, EMBS, WIE — each with their own executive committee and teams.

A person can be in multiple sub-branches at the same time. The design team lead of IAS might also be a member of WIE. The branch secretary is in all of it.

Fello understands this structure natively. It's not a generic project management tool that you have to bend into the right shape. It knows what an IEEE branch looks like. It knows the hierarchy. It knows who can see what.

And it generalizes — a Mozilla club with no sub-branches uses the same system. Just a single node with no children. Same intelligence, simpler shape.

## The Security Story

This is what makes Fello different from every other WhatsApp AI agent.

When companies and organizations look at tools like Hermes, their security teams say no. Their emails, their drives, their internal conversations going through a third party's system — it's a liability.

Fello answers that directly. Each organization owns their data. Each event gets its own dedicated WhatsApp number — not a shared gateway. Messages are stored in the organization's own database, not on a shared server. The design team member of IAS cannot see the publicity team's messages. The IAS chair cannot see the RAS chapter's data. The branch chair can see everything below them but cannot edit what sub-branches are doing.

Security is not a feature bolted on. It's the reason organizations can actually adopt this.

## The Expansion Story

It starts with IEEE student branches because that's where the research came from and the problem is understood from the inside.

Once Fello works for IEEE branches, the same platform works for any organization that wants it. The structure changes. The intelligence stays the same.
